## 1. Question and scope
How can an LLM serving system convert CXL-expanded KV-cache capacity into end-to-end serving performance? Focus:
cross-request KV prefix reuse in multi-turn serving (not KV offloading inside one decode). Four questions: **Q1** the real
end-to-end benefit of a host-tier (DRAM / CXL / SSD) KV hit; **Q2** how the existing scheduler handles requests that need
KV retrieval; **Q3** whether larger host capacity moves the bottleneck; **Q4** how much scheduling opportunity remains.
Constraints that held throughout: no change to vLLM or LMCache code (every configuration is a command-line flag), no DAX
/ firmware / system change, simulated and clairvoyant results never presented as measurements, the project's earlier
conclusions (RQ1, Phase 3) not touched, the branch never merged.

## 4. Results by family

### 4.1 A — what one host hit costs (`A/tables-a.md`, `A/figs/`; 8 repetitions per length, CUDA graphs, 8 output tokens)
- **Recompute (3B-AWQ):** 0.14 ms per token + 8.2 ns per token² of position (fit in `A/params-3b.json`, within 1 %): 45 ms at 256 tokens, 155 ms at 1k, 651 ms at 4k, 3.40 s at 16k; the 1.5B model is 1.9× faster.
- **DRAM host hit:** 19.4 / 22.5 / 34.8 / 51.7 / 85.1 ms TTFT at 256 / 1k / 4k / 8k / 16k tokens (repetitions within 1–2 ms). Stages (Table A-2): lookup 1.3 ms; host → GPU transfer at **11.3–12.0 GB/s = the PCIe 4.0 ×8 link** (3.4 ms per 1k tokens, 50 ms for the 604 MB of a 16k prefix); transfer end → scheduled 2.5–3.4 ms (finished loads are reported at the end of an engine step); the one-token step 10 ms (CUDA-graph floor; 20 ms eager). So TTFT(host hit) ≈ 16 ms + 3.4 ms per 1k tokens: cheaper than recompute from ≈ 100 tokens on, 7× at 1k, 19× at 4k, 40× at 16k.
- **GPU prefix hit:** 18.6–35 ms with LMCache in the loop against 11.8–40 ms for vLLM alone (12.8 vs 20.1 ms at 1k): the asynchronous lookup, a one-chunk (9.4 MB) load of the dropped partial block and the WAITING_FOR_REMOTE_KVS round trip add ≈ 7 ms to every repeat request.
- **SSD hit (NVMe L2, O_DIRECT; two runs):** 24 ms at 256 tokens, 37 at 1k, 65–110 at 4k, 136–170 at 8k, 281–318 ms at 16k — 1.9–3.7× a DRAM hit at 4k–16k, still 11–12× faster than recompute at 16k. The extra time is the L2 → L1 prefetch that runs *inside the lookup* at 2.7–4.9 GB/s (device rate 2.6–2.9 GiB/s), and it runs even for GPU-prefix hits (24 → 240 ms from 256 to 16k tokens) because the lookup precedes vLLM's decision. The first SSD run (`a-3b-ssd`) measured the page cache and is kept only as the "page cache" row; `a-3b-ssd-odirect` found its cold prompts already on disk (persistent L2 of the previous run) — its `host` rows are valid, the clean rerun `a-3b-ssd-odirect2` provides the cold rows.
- **Instrumentation controls:** hooks and samplers cost nothing measurable; the hook-less control was *slower* on isolated host hits because the GPU's idle-power ramp adds 30–40 ms after ≥ 50 ms of idleness (`a-3b-idle`, report §1.2); busy servers are unaffected.
- **What CXL changes (PREVIOUSLY MEASURED):** nothing on this path — the GPU reads devdax at the DRAM rate (12.48 GiB/s, 2026-10-07) and a devdax L1 gave the same TTFT (2026-10-11 / 10-18); the CPU-side differences (1.7× dependent-read latency, 51 vs 280 GiB/s streaming) are not on the GPU load path.

### 4.2 B — what happens to a hit under concurrency (`B/tables-b.md`, `B/figs/`; 40 GB DRAM L1, 32 output tokens)
- **Alone** (concurrency 1 / 0.5 req/s) a host hit costs 34 / 54 ms p50 against 319 / 653 ms for a cold request of the same length mix; a GPU hit 22 ms.
- **Under load the hit's TTFT advantage disappears:** closed loop at concurrency 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 host hits 363 / 766 / 1,879 / 3,428 ms p50 vs cold 329 / 731 / 1,519 / 3,159 ms; open loop at 1 / 2 / 4 req/s host 263 / 640 / 6,134 vs cold 652 / 995 / 5,420 ms (4 req/s is overloaded: GPU busy 1.0, 2.8 req/s completed); in the four-request burst the two host hits take 720–780 ms in every order = the cold request's 650 ms plus one step.
- **Where the time goes** (Table B-2, figure B4): the transfer is short and overlaps compute (16 / 39 / 318 ms at concurrency 2 / 4 / 16). The hit waits for (i) *load done → noticed*: finished loads are reported only at engine-step ends, and loaded steps are 2,048-token prefill chunks of other requests (300–600 ms) — 309 / 357 / 1,415 ms; (ii) *queued → load start*: the waiting-queue scan stops when the step's token budget is spent, so a request behind a cold prefill is not even looked up — 12 / 21 / 1,206 ms at concurrency 2 / 4 / 16, 4.4 s at 4 req/s; (iii) asynchronous scheduling submits the load one step after the request was considered (360 ms in the DCBA burst order). Once noticed, a loaded request is scheduled at the next step (0.1 ms) but shares that step with other requests' prefill chunks (scheduled → first token 21 / 37 / 295 ms instead of 10).
- **Knobs** (same scenarios, one flag each): eager prefetch — no change (host p50 within ±15 ms except concurrency 4); four transfer threads — no change; priority hits-first — host hits 766 → 433 ms at concurrency 4, 3,428 → 1,967 ms at 16 (−43 %), 6,134 → 3,079 ms at 4 req/s, cold requests +2–15 %; cold-first — the mirror image; prefill chunk 512 — step-granularity wait −50–80 % but the scan stops sooner, throughput −7–8 %, hits worse at high load (3,428 → 4,978 ms at concurrency 16); hits-first + chunk 512 — best hit TTFT at the highest loads (4 req/s: 2,459 ms vs 6,134 default) at +31 % for cold requests and −7–9 % throughput, worse than hits-first alone at moderate load; synchronous scheduling (`--no-async-scheduling`) — hits improve at every level (3,428 → 2,800 ms at concurrency 16, −10–20 %) at no throughput cost; vLLM alone (`b-none`) — the host tier adds ≈ 55 % of closed-loop capacity (1.5 → 2.3–2.4 completed req/s) and keeps up at 2 req/s where vLLM alone is overloaded (1.5 req/s, 12.9 s TTFT), yet closed-loop TTFT quantiles at concurrency 16 are *lower* without the cache (2,259 vs 3,336 ms: faster hits keep more cold prefill in flight); no GPU sampler — within noise.
- **Lookup latency under load:** 9–37 ms median at concurrency 2–16, 295 ms at 2 req/s, 31–340 ms in bursts (one CPU lookup worker, lookups queue behind the running prefill's stores).
- **Real-trace connector variants (BR, Table B-3):** eager prefetch and four transfer threads change nothing on either trace (every aggregate within 0.3 %, hit counts identical; `replay/tables-br-hitcat.md`). Eager moves the lookup only: the tool-agent host hits' lookup result is known 7.6 s after arrival instead of 9.8 s, the load is issued at 23.0 s in both runs — it waits for the block reservation of §4.5, not the lookup. The 4-thread run repeats the default to 34 ms per request, so the pair is the **repeatability measurement of the replay method: TTFT p50 within 0.02 %, p99 within 0.1 %**.

### 4.3 E — concurrent retrieval (`E/`, `E/figs/`; 16 host-resident prompts of 4k / 8k tokens = 151 / 302 MB of KV each)
- **DRAM, one engine:** k = 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 simultaneous 8k hits → TTFT p50 51 / 100 / 158 / 256 / 428 ms (max 719 ms); 11.9 → 7.7 GB/s over the burst — the loads serialise on LMCache's single transfer thread and stream per engine, not on the link. 4k: 35 / 77 / 105 / 157 / 257 ms.
- **Four transfer threads = one** (every number within 1 ms): LMCache routes one engine's requests to one worker (affinity by ZMQ identity); only a multi-engine server would use more.
- **SSD tier (O_DIRECT):** the in-lookup prefetch serialises on the NVMe at 3.3 GB/s: 110 → 1,453 ms for k = 1 … 16 (8k), TTFT p50 160 → 1,536 ms; beyond k ≈ 2 an 8k SSD hit costs more than a quarter of its recompute (1.43 s).
- **Loads beside prefill (e2):** k hits + k 4k cold prompts → hit TTFT 702 / 1,355 / 1,692 / 1,759 ms (k = 1 / 2 / 4 / 8): the loads finish in tens of ms but are served only after the running prefill chunks. **Beside decode (e3):** 155 / 214 ms instead of 69 / 86 ms at k = 4 / 8 (noticed at decode-step boundaries, then shares a decoding step).
- **No CPU staging** on the DRAM path (the transfer kernel reads the pinned objects directly; `E/*-cpu.txt` on /mnt/local).

### 4.4 C — how far the scheduler is from a reference (SIMULATED; `C/c-summary.csv`, `C/c-decomposition.csv`, `C/figs/`)
- **Model:** `benchmarks/serving/sched_sim.py`, a discrete-event model of one engine replaying B's measured sequences (arrivals, lengths, tier served, bytes): steps of ≤ 2,048 prefill tokens at the measured per-token cost (r₀ 0.139 ms, r₁ 8.2 ns/token²), 10.2 ms minimum step, 1.26 ms per sequence per step of decode, one transfer channel at 11.9 GB/s, 1.3 ms lookup, 3.2 ms hand-over, loads noticed at step boundaries, 16 sequences. **Validation:** the model of the existing scheduler is 7–27 % optimistic against the measured default (711 vs 969 ms mean TTFT at concurrency 4; 3,115 vs 3,477 at 16; 4,694 vs 5,547 at 4 req/s) and reproduces the direction and most of the size of every measured knob.
- **Opportunity at concurrency 16** (mean TTFT of the model): scanning past the budget −23 %; submitting loads at once a further −1 %; zero-delay loads under the existing scan only −10 % (ZERO-DELAY); hits first −29 %; clairvoyant SRPT −42 % (−60 % under overload, where it raises p95 by starving long cold prompts) (CLAIRVOYANT). **The budget-limited scan is the larger load-side problem, load latency the smaller.** At concurrency ≤ 2 or ≤ 0.5 req/s every reference is within 1–5 %: nothing to recover. The measured knobs land where the model puts them (synchronous −14 %; hits-first −43 % for the hits, −12 % overall).

### 4.5 D — capacity under overload, trace seconds 0–100 at 0.08× (`replay/tables-d.md`, `tables-d-tool.md`, `tables-d-hitcat.md`; figures D1–D4)
Replay: `benchmarks/serving/replay.py`; each 512-token hash block of the trace becomes a fixed pseudo-random token sequence (shared blocks = shared prefixes), prompts cut at ≈ 31.7k tokens, outputs from the trace capped at 1,024; 1,250–1,290 wall seconds per run; all requests of every run completed.
- **Conversation window** (291 requests, 3.34 M prompt tokens, 0.22 req/s, 2,560 offered prompt tokens/s): overloaded — GPU busy 0.88, backlog up to 26 against 16 decode slots, TTFT p50 16 s / p99 56 s, queue → scheduled p50 10–11 s. Capacity does nothing up to 40 GB (host share 0.000–0.004; the window offers no reuse beyond the GPU prefix cache's 4.4 %); at 80 GB the host tier serves 2.9 % of the prompt tokens, removes 2 % of the prefill work and lowers TTFT p50 by 11 % (16.0 → 14.3 s), p99 by 14 %, request completion time by 17 % (43 → 36 s): a saturated queue amplifies a small reduction of service demand. SSD-backed lands in between (host share 1.3 %, 14.5 s; its L2 never evicts). With nothing to serve the LMCache configurations are 2.5 % slower than vLLM alone (16.0 vs 15.6 s) while storing 77 GB per window. Host hits are *not* faster than misses here (80 GB: 16.9 vs 15.0 s p50) while GPU-prefix hits are (12.9 vs 16.8 s). The four no-hit configurations agree within 0.4 % on TTFT p50.
- **Tool-agent window** (544 requests, 4.61 M tokens, 0.42 req/s, 3,560 tokens/s): overloaded too (busy 0.87–0.89, backlog 41–46) but short-distance reuse makes capacity pay from 2 GB: cached share 0.256 (vLLM alone, GPU prefix only) → 0.312 (2 GB) → 0.323 (5) → 0.332 (10–20) → 0.335 (40) → 0.354 (80 GB), within one point of the offline LRU; computed prefill −10 % at 80 GB (2,586 → 2,322 tok/s); TTFT p50 18.5 → 15.8 → 14.6 → 13.2 → 12.9 → 12.7 s (**−31 % at 80 GB**), p95 −30 %, p99 −34 %, request time −28 % (32.5 → 23.5 s); 5 GB already gives −21 %. Stores are 75–76 GB per window at every capacity (LMCache stores every prompt), so even the 80 GB tier evicts (1,455 chunks; 7,524 at 5 GB). The SSD-backed tier finds *fewer* hits than 5 GB of DRAM (host share 5.7 vs 8.4 %; losses late in the window; cause not isolated — candidates: the asynchronous L2 store path and the bounded in-lookup prefetch).
- **Which requests the tier helps (§7.2c, `tables-d-hitcat.md`, `hitcat_d.py`):** grouped by prefix source (host-dominant ≥ 50 % retrieved, GPU-prefix ≥ 50 %, partial 10–50 %, miss < 10 %), **host-dominant hits are the slowest class in every run with host hits** — tool-agent 80 GB: 21.0 s p50 (n 95) vs 6.8 s GPU-prefix hits (215) vs 13.8 s misses (167) of twice the length; at equal backlog at arrival (< 20 outstanding) 16.8 / 2.7 / 11.5 s; conversation 80 GB 23.8 / 11.9 / 14.4 s. The transfer takes 0.09 s, the wait for the finished load 0.9 s; the time sits **between the lookup result and the moment the load is issued**: 7.1 s p50 (12.5 s p50 / 26 s p90 among the 57 of 95 hits with a gap > 2 s). **Mechanism (SOURCE INSPECTION, `vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py`, vLLM 0.31):** an asynchronous external load is issued only after `allocate_slots` reserves GPU blocks for *all* external tokens at once (minus the in-flight prefills' reservations); if that fails the waiting scan `break`s with no fallback to computing the prefix; a miss is admitted with one 2,048-token chunk and grows as a running request, served before the waiting queue is scanned. Under GPU-KV saturation (5–11 running sequences fill the 121,792-token cache) a 6k-token whole-prefix reservation is the hardest allocation in the system, so the requests the tier could serve in 90 ms are admitted last. The capacity benefit under overload is therefore entirely the work the hits remove, never the hits being fast.

### 4.6 DL — capacity at a sustainable load, trace seconds 0–200 at 0.04× (`replay/tables-dl-conv.md`, `tables-dl-tool.md`, `tables-dl-hitcat.md`; figures D1–D4 `-dl`)
Half the replay speed, twice the window (same reuse distances in trace time, twice the requests), plus a 320 GB tier that holds the whole window's reuse for both traces (offline LRU: conversation 0.183 at 320 GB = unbounded; tool-agent 0.447 = unbounded). 4,982 wall s per run; every request completed.
- **Conversation** (599 requests, 6.85 M tokens, 0.12 req/s, 1,382 tokens/s): not saturated — GPU busy 0.49–0.52, backlog ≤ 16, queue → scheduled 0.1–0.2 s. Cached share 0.044 (2 GB = GPU prefix only = vLLM alone) → 0.088 (80 GB) → **0.164 (320 GB; offline 0.183)**; prefill work −12.5 %; TTFT p50 4.30 → 3.98 → **3.46 s (−19 %)**, p95 −9 %, p99 −10 %, request time 17.8 → 15.9 s (−11 %). **Host-dominant hits are now the fastest requests:** 1.2 s p50 at 80 GB (n 28), **0.8 s at 320 GB (n 65)** against 5.2–5.4 s for misses (13.7–13.9k tokens, 3 s of prefill) and 1.1–1.3 s for GPU-prefix hits — inside a hit the retrieval path costs what A measured (lookup 20–160 ms, load noticed 0.1 s after issue, 60–70 ms transfer, 0.2 s tail prefill). Cost side: the 2 GB tier matches vLLM alone within 0.7 % while storing 233 GB per window and evicting 24,488 chunks (16,006 at 80 GB; 0 at 320 GB, L1 peak 198 GB). Residues: a sixth of the host hits still wait > 2 s between lookup result and load inside bursts (p50 7–9 s), so the tail improves less than the median.

## 5. What the families say together (report §9, executive summary)
1. **A host-tier hit is cheap and the transfer never limits anything.** 19–85 ms per hit alone (12 GB/s + ≈ 16 ms fixed); retrieval is < 0.2 % of the link's time per replay window; simultaneous hits serialise on LMCache's one transfer thread per engine, not on bandwidth. Nothing in the study depends on the tier's medium.
2. **What capacity buys depends on the regime.** Under overload it is prefill work removed from a saturated GPU, amplified by the queue for every request (conversation 80 GB: 2.9 % host-served tokens → TTFT p50 −11 %; tool-agent 80 GB: −10 % work → −31 %), while the hits themselves are the slowest requests. At a sustainable load it is the hits' own speed plus shorter bursts: 320 GB → TTFT p50 −19 % (conversation) / −36 % (tool-agent), request time −11 / −21 %, p99 −10 / −17 %. A tier with nothing to serve costs nothing measurable (2 GB = vLLM alone within 1 %).
3. **How much a capacity buys is a property of the trace, and predictable** (§4.8): short-distance reuse (tool-agent) is mostly captured by 5 GB; long-distance reuse (conversation) needs CXL-scale capacity.
4. **The scheduler's weakness is admission, not retrieval.** Whole-prefix block reservation before an asynchronous load, misses admitted chunk by chunk, no fallback on a failed reservation (SOURCE INSPECTION): host-dominant hits are the slowest class under GPU-KV saturation — overloaded (16.8 s vs 2.7 s GPU-prefix hits vs 11.5 s misses at equal backlog) and inside the bursts of a sustainable load (3.3 s vs 0.3 s; a third wait 13 s between lookup result and load).
5. **The other scheduling costs are generic and bounded:** budget-limited queue scan, step-boundary notice of a finished load, one-step-late submission under asynchronous scheduling, FCFS ordering — 20–40 % of mean TTFT at high load in the calibrated model, nothing at light load; synchronous scheduling recovers −14 %, hits-first −43 % for the hits; eager prefetch and transfer threads do nothing.
6. **LMCache stores every prompt's KV** — 75–99 GB per 100 trace-seconds, 188–237 GB per 200, 57 GB in a 7-minute controlled run — so a 40 GB tier evicts its own working set; what the tier holds (admission, RQ1) decides its value.

## 6. Answers to the brief's five questions (report §12)
- **Q1 — meaningful end-to-end benefit?** Yes, measured in both regimes: latency for the hits at light load (19–85 ms vs 45–3,402 ms per request); capacity at saturation for everyone in proportion to the work removed (−31 % TTFT p50 and −28 % request time, tool-agent 80 GB; 0.6 → 1.9 req/s with 8k prompts when every request hits; +55 % closed-loop capacity); at a sustainable load −19 / −36 % TTFT p50 at 320 GB. The CXL-specific part is capacity for long-distance reuse (conversation: 5 % of the hour reusable at 4.5 GB, 13 % at 80 GB, 31 % at 320 GB, 37 % at 640 GB); its data path is PREVIOUSLY MEASURED equal to DRAM, the DAX devices were blocked, and the magnitudes are those of a 3B model on a small GPU.
- **Q2 — what prevents full exploitation of cached prefixes?** Ranked: misses (84 % / 57 % of prompt tokens still computed at 320 GB — first occurrences that only longer-lived capacity turns into hits) > scheduling under load (budget-limited scan, step-boundary notice, one-step-late submission, whole-prefix reservation) > software overheads (≈ 16 ms fixed per hit, one transfer thread per engine, store-everything, in-lookup L2 prefetch) > the transfer. CXL bandwidth does not appear.
- **Q3 — is the existing scheduler effective?** At light load yes (within 1–5 % of the feasible references; never blocks the GPU on a load; serves a loaded request at the next step). Under load and inside bursts no: its own knobs do not fix the four mechanisms; the opportunity is 20–40 % of mean TTFT at high load (SIMULATED), of which measured flags recover part (synchronous −14 %; hits-first −43 % for the hits); the admission penalty has no reference bound because no policy was implemented — the GPU-prefix-hit category (0.3 s vs 3.3 s; 2.7 s vs 16.8 s) is the natural target.
- **Q4 — a distinct CXL systems problem?** General serving issues (scan, hand-over, ordering, GPU-KV-bound admission) and generic tiered-caching issues (store-everything, LRU vs short-distance reuse with an SSD L2, in-lookup prefetch, one transfer thread) are present with any tier; issues that *grow* with CXL's capacity are the admission path being taken by many requests (148 of 1,159 at 320 GB vs 15 at 2 GB), reuse-aware ordering's value, store / eviction volume; issues caused by CXL's own memory characteristics: **none measured** on this platform (the GPU reads devdax at the DRAM rate; the one candidate — a GPU link faster than the CXL pair's ≈ 50 GiB/s — cannot be produced here).
- **Q5 — what to build next, ranked (nothing implemented):** (1) a vLLM admission policy for external loads under GPU-KV saturation — chunked external loads, a bounded reservation for pending loads, or compute-when-reservation-fails (strongest evidence: MEASURED on both traces in both regimes + SOURCE INSPECTION; no new data path); (2) reuse-aware queue scan, ordering and hand-over (generic; 20–40 % SIMULATED; Bidaw, FAST '26, already orders by KV-loading latency and size, so the overlap must be checked mechanism by mechanism); (3) CXL-scale capacity for long-distance reuse — the only CXL-specific lever, MEASURED −19 / −36 % at 320 GB, needing the DAX path and RQ1's admission rather than a new mechanism; (4) tier-aware lookup and prefetch for SSD-backed tiers. Where the system already works well (light load; sustainable load with independent arrivals) no direction applies.

## 7. Unexpected and negative results (report §10, condensed)
- A host hit stops being faster than a miss as soon as the server is loaded; under GPU-KV saturation host-dominant hits are the slowest requests of all, and the condition is saturation at arrival (bursts), not overload.
- Eager prefetch does nothing measurable (moves the lookup, not the load); four transfer threads equal one (affinity routing); priority ordering only moves the wait between classes; prefill chunk 512 trades one wait for another and loses at high load; synchronous scheduling is the one knob that helped everywhere (−10–20 %, free).
- The measured "lookup time" under load (≈ 0.5 s) is the scheduler's polling interval, not LMCache (10–20 ms when the engine is idle; same in runs with and without eviction).
- The host tier costs something with nothing to serve: ≈ 7 ms per GPU-prefix hit, 2.5 % TTFT p50 under overload, 77–237 GB of stores per window.
- Closed-loop TTFT quantiles are *lower* without the host tier at concurrency 16 although the tier adds ≈ 55 % of capacity; the metric must match the regime.
- 100-second trace windows carry almost no capacity-sensitive reuse (conversation 4.5 → 8.5 % at 80 GB); capacity experiments need long windows or a trace-driven cache model, not faster replay — this is why the 0–200 s sweep at 0.04× replaced the planned 0–50 s mini-sweep.
- The replayed load is bound by GPU KV capacity, not compute (5–11 of 16 slots running when requests wait); a host hit frees compute but not GPU blocks.
- A 40 GB host tier is exhausted by a 7-minute controlled run (57 GB stored); the SSD-backed tier found fewer short-distance hits than 5 GB of DRAM on the tool-agent trace (cause not isolated) but more than 40 GB of DRAM on the conversation trace.
- The first SSD runs measured the page cache, then the previous run's disk (fixed with O_DIRECT and per-run L2 directories); the hook-less control was slower on isolated hits (GPU idle-power ramp); vLLM alone moves 20–30 GB of PCIe traffic per direction per run with no KV tier (unexplained baseline — KV traffic is therefore taken from LMCache's events, not the PCIe counters).
- Run-hygiene incidents (all recorded, none changes a conclusion; `experiment-matrix.md`, report §15.C): the first `b-default` re-used cold prompts across levels; `b-noasync` truncated when `/home` hit its 40 GB quota on 2026-11-04 20:55 (results moved to `/mnt/local`); a `KeyError` in the first priority runs; a few "Server disconnected" answers to 16,320-token flush fillers inside 16-request bursts; the lighter-load queue starting early and contaminating `d-tool-dram2` (re-run 2026-11-05 05:42–06:04); `smokee` failing on an `IndexError` in the harness.

## 8. Limitations and blockers (report §11)
- **CXL was not exercised** (DAX root-only since 2026-10-18; admin action required). Every capacity point is pinned DRAM; the transfer to CXL rests on the PREVIOUSLY MEASURED equalities of §2; CXL's host-side interference and its CPU-side latency for LMCache's own work are not represented.
- **One small GPU, one small model:** the 4.5 GB GPU KV cache binds concurrency at the replayed loads; an H100 with a 70B model has ≈ 9× the bytes per token, 10× the KV capacity and a 4× faster link, so the magnitudes do not transfer — the mechanisms (asynchronous lookup / load, step-boundary notice, budget-bounded scan, one transfer thread per engine, blocks needed by a hit) are properties of the engine and do.
- **Trace replay is time-scaled and synthetic:** 100 / 200 trace-seconds at 0.08× / 0.04× keep the sequence and reuse structure but stretch inter-arrival times and truncate reuse distance; prompts are pseudo-random token ids built from the hash blocks; outputs capped at 1,024 tokens; one replay per configuration (repeatability 0.02 % on TTFT p50; window-to-window variance not measured).
- **The scheduling reference is a model** (7–27 % optimistic); its zero-delay and clairvoyant variants are bounds, not policies.
- **Software versions:** vLLM 0.31.0 (asynchronous scheduling on by default), LMCache 0.5.5 multi-process mode; later versions may move the step-boundary notice or the affinity routing.
- **Scope:** single engine, no tensor parallelism, no prefill–decode disaggregation, no multi-tenant host memory, no real users.
- **Operational:** the home quota filled on 2026-11-04 20:55 (older account content, not this study's 0.6 GB at the time); runs wrote to `/mnt/local` behind a symlink; at the end the small artifacts were copied here and the bulky per-run logs stayed on c28's local NVMe (§10 below) — a documented departure from the convention of every raw byte under `experimental_results/`.

## 9. Relationship to the project's earlier results (report §13; conclusions unchanged)
RQ1 (reuse-aware admission) stays relevant and gains a reason: LMCache stores every prompt, so what the tier holds decides
its value; the adaptive admission policy was *not* tested in the serving loop (no claim either way). Capacity above the
working set makes admission matter less for the hit rate (the 160 GB re-run of F; 320 GB captures 90–96 % of the offline
reuse of the 200-s windows) but not for the write traffic, which is the larger flow at every capacity and the interference
channel of Phase 3. The Phase 2 / 3 bandwidth and interference measurements do not predict serving behaviour on this GPU
path (12 GB/s link, 60–80 MB/s of stores against GB/s writers); they remain the CPU-side characterisation they were. The
negative joint-benefit conclusion of Phase 3 (H2 not supported) holds — nothing here bears on it. The three RQs are not
redefined; the study adds a serving-level characterisation: under GPU-KV saturation the serving-side bottleneck is vLLM's
admission of external loads, and the measured 200-s windows plus the trace analysis say where CXL-scale capacity matters
(long-distance reuse) and how much.

## 10. Recommended next experiment (executive summary of the report)
Put the smallest admission change — a bounded block reservation for pending external loads, or chunked loading — behind
a flag in a vLLM fork and replay the tool-agent window at 0.04× and 0.08× with the 320 GB tier; prediction: host-dominant
hits fall from 3.3 s / 16.8 s p50 toward the GPU-prefix-hit category's 0.3 s / 2.7 s with no loss for misses. Then repeat
on an 80-GB-class GPU (c33 / c29) with a larger model to check that GPU-KV saturation, not this GPU's 4.5 GB, drives the
effect. Prerequisite for the CXL part: the DAX permissions (admin), after which the 320 GB point is re-run on the devdax L1
(expected identical, PREVIOUSLY MEASURED).
